What's Different with Concierge Medicine? Everything You Need to Know

Tired of rushed appointments and long wait times? Discover how concierge medicine works differently — and why patients in Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin, and Granite Bay are making the switch.

← Back to articles

Most people have had the experience: you wait weeks for an appointment, sit in a waiting room for 45 minutes, and then see your doctor for less than 10. You leave with a prescription and more questions than answers. It's not anyone's fault — it's how the conventional healthcare system is designed. Doctors in traditional practices manage anywhere from 1,500 to 3,000 patients at a time. There simply isn't room for anything more.

Concierge medicine is built on an entirely different model. And for a growing number of patients in Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin, Granite Bay, and surrounding communities, it's changing what it means to have a doctor.

What Is Concierge Medicine?

Concierge medicine (also called membership-based medicine or direct primary care) is a healthcare model in which patients pay a monthly or annual membership fee directly to their physician. In exchange, the doctor limits the size of their practice — typically to 150–600 patients instead of the conventional 1,500–3,000 — and provides a dramatically higher level of access, attention, and personalized care.

The membership fee covers most primary care services directly, outside of insurance. Some concierge practices still bill insurance for labs, imaging, and specialist referrals; others operate entirely outside the insurance system. Either way, the core relationship between you and your doctor is direct, personal, and unhurried.

Think of it as the difference between flying coach and flying private — both get you to the same destination, but the experience, the attention, and the outcomes are fundamentally different.

How Concierge Medicine Differs from Traditional Healthcare

Understanding concierge medicine is easiest when you compare it side by side with what most people are used to.

Appointment Availability

Traditional medicine: New patient appointments often take 3–6 weeks. Same-day or next-day availability is rare. Urgent issues frequently push patients to urgent care or the ER.

Concierge medicine: Same-day and next-day appointments are standard. Many concierge physicians offer same-day access as a core benefit of membership. Urgent needs are addressed by your actual doctor — not a stranger at a walk-in clinic.

Time with Your Doctor

Traditional medicine: The average primary care visit in the U.S. lasts 15–18 minutes. With documentation, intake, and checkout, you may get 8–10 minutes of face-to-face time with your physician.

Concierge medicine: Appointments typically run 30–60 minutes — or longer for complex cases. There's no clock running, no quota to hit. Your doctor can actually listen, think, and spend time on what matters.

Direct Access

Traditional medicine: Reaching your doctor directly is nearly impossible. You call a main line, speak to a receptionist, leave a message, and wait for a call back — often from a nurse rather than the physician.

Concierge medicine: Most concierge physicians provide their personal cell phone number or a direct messaging portal. You can text or call your doctor directly with questions, concerns, or results. This alone changes everything for patients managing chronic conditions or navigating complex health decisions.

Personalized, Preventive Focus

Traditional medicine: The system is optimized to treat illness reactively — you come in sick, you get diagnosed and treated, you leave. Preventive care and wellness optimization often fall through the cracks due to time constraints.

Concierge medicine: With more time and fewer patients, concierge physicians can actually practice the kind of proactive, preventive medicine most doctors went to medical school hoping to provide. Annual comprehensive wellness evaluations, personalized health plans, and ongoing optimization — not just problem-solving — become the norm.

Continuity of Care

Traditional medicine: Many patients see a different provider every visit, especially in large group practices. Your health history is in a chart, but the relationship — and the contextual knowledge that comes with it — is often missing.

Concierge medicine: You see the same physician every time. Over months and years, your doctor develops a deep understanding of your health, your lifestyle, your goals, and your patterns. That continuity leads to better decisions and catches things that would otherwise be missed.

What's Typically Included in a Concierge Medicine Membership

While offerings vary by practice, most concierge medicine memberships include:

  • Comprehensive annual wellness exam — far more thorough than a standard physical, often including advanced lab panels, cardiovascular risk assessment, body composition analysis, and a detailed review of your health goals
  • Same-day or next-day appointments for acute concerns
  • Unlimited or near-unlimited office visits — no copays per visit within the membership
  • Direct physician access via phone, text, or secure messaging
  • Extended appointment times with no rush
  • Coordination of specialist care — your doctor actively manages referrals and follows up on outcomes
  • After-hours access for urgent questions
  • Telehealth visits — especially valuable for patients in Roseville, Rocklin, and Granite Bay who may not want to travel for a simple follow-up

Many concierge practices in the Sacramento area also integrate functional medicine, hormone optimization, IV therapy, peptide therapy, medical weight loss, and other advanced wellness services — creating a truly comprehensive health partnership under one roof.

Who Is Concierge Medicine For?

Concierge medicine was once associated exclusively with executives and the ultra-wealthy. That perception is outdated. Membership fees have come down significantly, and for many patients, the value — both financial and in terms of health outcomes — is compelling across income levels.

Concierge medicine tends to be a particularly strong fit for:

  • Busy professionals and executives — who can't afford to take a half-day for a doctor's appointment, need same-day answers, and value direct access to a trusted physician
  • Patients with chronic conditions — diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disease, autoimmune conditions, and others who benefit enormously from having a doctor who truly knows their case and is proactive about management
  • Families — parents who want a physician who actually knows their children and can be reached quickly, not a different provider at every visit
  • Active adults and athletes — who want performance optimization, faster recovery support, and a doctor who understands their fitness goals, not just their illness history
  • People approaching or in their 50s, 60s, and beyond — who are thinking seriously about longevity, preventive screening, and maintaining their quality of life as they age
  • Anyone who has been frustrated by the traditional system — long waits, rushed visits, feeling like a number rather than a person

In communities like Granite Bay, Rocklin, and Roseville — where residents tend to be health-conscious, professionally accomplished, and accustomed to high-quality service — concierge medicine is a natural fit.

The Real Cost of Concierge Medicine (It May Be Less Than You Think)

Concierge membership fees typically range from $150 to $500 per month per adult, depending on the practice, services included, and geographic area. Many practices offer family rates or reduced fees for children.

At first glance, that may seem like an added expense on top of insurance. But consider the true cost comparison:

  • Urgent care visits — typically $150–$250 per visit. If you go twice a year for issues your concierge doctor would handle directly, that's $300–$500.
  • ER visits for non-emergencies — often $500–$2,000+. Concierge patients route far fewer issues to the ER because they can reach their doctor directly.
  • Specialist visits — because concierge physicians have more time to manage complex cases themselves, many issues that would otherwise require a specialist can be handled in-house, reducing downstream costs.
  • Lost productivity — the time cost of waiting weeks for an appointment, sitting in a waiting room, and managing health issues without direct physician guidance is real and significant.

For many patients in Sacramento and the surrounding area, the membership fee pays for itself in avoided urgent care visits and specialist referrals within the first year.

It's also worth noting that Health Savings Account (HSA) and Flexible Spending Account (FSA) funds can often be used to pay concierge membership fees, depending on how the practice is structured. Ask your prospective provider about this.

Concierge Medicine vs. Direct Primary Care: What's the Difference?

These terms are often used interchangeably, but there is a distinction:

Concierge medicine typically operates alongside insurance. The membership fee covers enhanced access and services, but the practice still bills insurance for office visits, labs, and procedures. Patients generally need to maintain their insurance coverage.

Direct Primary Care (DPC) operates entirely outside of insurance. The membership fee covers all primary care services with no co-pays or insurance billing. DPC practices tend to have lower membership fees and are paired with high-deductible, low-premium insurance plans for catastrophic coverage.

Both models offer the same core benefits — smaller patient panels, more time, direct access, and personalized care. The right fit depends on your insurance situation, health needs, and budget. A consultation with a concierge or DPC physician in the Sacramento area can help you determine which model makes the most sense.

What to Look for in a Concierge Doctor in Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin & Granite Bay

If you're considering making the switch, here's what to evaluate:

  • Panel size — ask how many patients the physician currently serves. Smaller is generally better for access and attention.
  • Access terms — can you text or call the doctor directly? What's the average response time? Is after-hours contact available?
  • Services included — what's covered in the membership vs. billed separately? Are advanced wellness services like hormone optimization, IV therapy, or functional medicine available?
  • Philosophy of care — is the practice focused on reactive treatment, or does it take a proactive, preventive, whole-patient approach? The best concierge physicians see themselves as partners in your long-term health, not just treaters of illness.
  • Fit and communication style — this is your doctor. You should feel heard, respected, and comfortable being honest about your health and lifestyle.

Why Patients in Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin & Granite Bay Are Making the Switch

The Sacramento region is home to some of the most discerning, health-forward patients in Northern California. Residents in Granite Bay and Roseville are accustomed to high-quality service and personalized experiences in every other area of their lives — and increasingly, they're expecting the same from their healthcare.

At the same time, the Sacramento Valley's lifestyle creates real healthcare needs: the stress of high-achieving careers, the physical demands of an active outdoor lifestyle, the complexity of managing health for a family. A doctor who knows you — really knows you — and is available when you need them isn't a luxury in that context. It's a logical choice.

Concierge medicine in Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin, and Granite Bay is growing for a simple reason: it works. Patients get better care, catch problems earlier, spend less time in waiting rooms, and feel genuinely supported in their health — not processed through a system.

Is Concierge Medicine Right for You?

If you've ever left a doctor's appointment feeling rushed, unheard, or uncertain — or if you've delayed seeking care because getting an appointment takes too long — concierge medicine is worth a serious look.

The best way to find out if it's the right fit is to schedule a meet-and-greet with a concierge physician in your area. Most practices offer a complimentary consultation so you can ask questions, see the facility, and get a feel for the doctor's approach before committing.

You deserve a doctor who knows your name, has time for your questions, and is invested in keeping you healthy — not just treating you when you're sick.
