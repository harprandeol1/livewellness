What Should You Expect from Your First IV Therapy Session? A Complete Guide

Curious about IV therapy but not sure what to expect? Here's exactly what happens before, during, and after your first IV drip — and why patients in Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin, and Granite Bay are making it part of their wellness routine.

← Back to articles

If you've been hearing more about IV therapy lately — from friends, wellness blogs, or your provider — you might be curious but a little unsure about what it actually involves. Is it uncomfortable? How long does it take? What will you feel afterward?

The good news: your first IV therapy session is far simpler and more comfortable than most people expect. This guide walks you through everything — before, during, and after your first drip — so you can walk in confident and get the most out of the experience.

What Is IV Therapy and Why Are People Doing It?

IV (intravenous) therapy delivers vitamins, minerals, amino acids, antioxidants, and hydration directly into your bloodstream — bypassing the digestive system entirely. When you take supplements orally, your body absorbs only a fraction of the nutrients due to digestive processing. With IV therapy, absorption is close to 100%, which means faster, more noticeable results.

People pursue IV therapy for a wide range of reasons, including:

  • Energy and fatigue recovery — addressing chronic low energy, burnout, or adrenal fatigue
  • Immune support — high-dose vitamin C and zinc IVs to fight illness or prevent it
  • Hydration — rapid rehydration after illness, intense exercise, heat exposure, or travel
  • Hangover recovery — one of the most requested single-session uses
  • Athletic performance and recovery — amino acids, B vitamins, and magnesium for muscle repair
  • Anti-aging and skin health — glutathione, NAD+, and collagen-supporting nutrients
  • Migraine and headache relief — magnesium IVs have strong clinical support for migraines
  • Mental clarity and focus — NAD+ therapy in particular has gained popularity for cognitive performance
  • Weight loss support — specific formulas include lipotropic compounds to support fat metabolism

For residents throughout Sacramento, Roseville, Rocklin, and Granite Bay, IV therapy has become a practical wellness tool — not a luxury. Whether you're a busy parent running on empty, an athlete pushing your limits, or a professional managing high demands, IV therapy offers a fast, effective way to restore what your body is missing.

Before Your First IV Session: What to Do to Prepare

A little preparation goes a long way toward making your first experience as comfortable and effective as possible.

Eat Something Beforehand

Arriving on an empty stomach can increase the likelihood of lightheadedness or nausea, especially with certain nutrient-dense formulas. Have a light meal or snack 30–60 minutes before your appointment. Nothing heavy — just enough to have something in your system.

Hydrate Before You Arrive

IV therapy adds fluids and nutrients to your system, but starting well-hydrated makes it easier to place the IV line and helps your body utilize nutrients more effectively. Drink at least 16 oz of water before your appointment.

Wear Comfortable, Loose-Fitting Clothing

You'll be seated or reclining during your session, so comfort matters. Wear a short-sleeved shirt or something with sleeves that can easily roll up past the elbow — that's typically where the IV line is placed.

Share Your Health History

Before your first session, your provider will conduct a brief intake and health screening. Be ready to share any medications you're taking, known allergies (especially to vitamins or supplements), recent illnesses, and any chronic health conditions. This helps your provider choose the right formula and flag any potential interactions.

Arrive Without Rushing

Give yourself a few extra minutes. Feeling rushed or anxious can cause your veins to constrict slightly, making placement a little harder. Arriving calm and a little early sets the tone for the whole session.

During Your First IV Therapy Session: Step by Step

Step 1: Intake and Consultation (5–10 Minutes)

Your first session will start with a brief health screening. A nurse or provider will review your intake form, ask about your goals and any symptoms, check your blood pressure, and confirm which IV formula is right for you. This is also the time to ask any questions — don't hesitate.

First-timers often ask: "Will it hurt?" The honest answer: there's a small pinch when the needle is inserted, comparable to a blood draw. Once the catheter is in place and the needle is removed, most people feel nothing at all.

Step 2: IV Placement (2–5 Minutes)

A trained nurse or medical professional will clean the insertion site (typically the inside of your elbow or the back of your hand) and place a small catheter — a thin, flexible tube — into your vein. The needle used to guide it is removed immediately after placement; only the soft catheter stays in.

If your veins are harder to access, your provider may apply a warm compress or ask you to make a fist a few times to bring them up. Experienced IV nurses handle this routinely.

Step 3: The Infusion (30–90 Minutes)

Once the line is placed, your drip begins. How long it takes depends on the formula and the volume being infused. A standard hydration drip might run 30–45 minutes; a NAD+ infusion or high-dose vitamin C can take 60–90 minutes or more.

During this time, you're free to:

  • Relax in a comfortable chair or recliner
  • Use your phone, read, or watch something
  • Chat with staff or other patients
  • Rest or even nap

Most patients describe the experience as genuinely relaxing. The infusion room is typically quiet and comfortable — think of it as a productive rest.

What you might feel during the infusion:

  • A cool or slightly cold sensation moving up your arm — this is normal and passes quickly. Letting staff know allows them to slow the drip rate slightly.
  • A flushing or warm sensation — common with B vitamins and magnesium; harmless
  • A slight taste in your mouth — some patients notice a metallic or vitamin-like taste, especially with B12 or certain mineral infusions
  • General relaxation or calmness — many patients feel noticeably at ease within minutes

Step 4: Line Removal and Recovery (5 Minutes)

When the drip is complete, the catheter is removed quickly and painlessly, and a small bandage is placed on the site. You may sit for a few minutes before standing, especially if this is your first session.

After Your First IV Session: What to Expect

Immediate Effects

Many patients notice something within minutes to hours of finishing their infusion. The most commonly reported immediate effects include:

  • Increased energy — often described as clean, calm energy rather than a caffeine-like spike
  • Mental clarity — sharper focus and reduced brain fog
  • Improved mood — especially with formulas containing magnesium, B vitamins, or amino acids
  • Reduced headache or muscle tension — particularly after magnesium or hydration-focused drips
  • General sense of wellbeing — many patients simply feel better, lighter, more themselves

Some effects are more gradual — improved sleep over the following nights, better skin tone over several days, or cumulative immune benefits over a series of sessions.

You May Feel Tired

Counterintuitively, some first-time patients feel pleasantly tired after their session — particularly after NAD+ or detox-focused formulas. This is normal. Your body is processing and utilizing a significant influx of nutrients. Rest if your body calls for it; many people sleep exceptionally well the night of their infusion.

Drink Water

Continue hydrating after your session. IV fluids support hydration, but your kidneys will be working to process the nutrients and any byproducts. Extra water helps flush these efficiently.

Your Urine May Look Different

This surprises some first-timers: if your formula included B vitamins (especially B2/riboflavin), your urine may be a bright yellow or neon color for several hours. This is completely normal and simply reflects the water-soluble vitamins being excreted. It's not a sign that the IV "didn't work."

When Will You Feel Results?

This varies by person, formula, and what you came in for:

  • Hangover, dehydration, migraine — most patients feel dramatically better within 1–2 hours of completing the drip
  • Energy and fatigue — often noticeable the same day or by the following morning
  • Immune support — benefits build over 24–48 hours and with repeated sessions
  • Anti-aging, skin, and longevity (NAD+, glutathione) — most meaningful results come from a series of sessions over weeks

Common Questions About Your First IV Session

Is IV therapy safe? Yes — when administered by trained medical professionals using pharmacy-grade nutrients, IV therapy has an excellent safety record. It's important to always choose a medically supervised clinic rather than an unregulated "pop-up" IV bar.

Can anyone get IV therapy? Most healthy adults are good candidates. However, people with kidney disease, heart conditions, certain blood disorders, or specific allergies may need modified formulas or may not be candidates. This is why an intake screening before your first session is essential.

How often should I get IV therapy? It depends on your goals. For general wellness maintenance, many patients in Roseville, Rocklin, and Sacramento opt for once or twice a month. For targeted goals like NAD+ therapy or immune support during illness, a more frequent initial series is often recommended.

Does insurance cover IV therapy? Most wellness IV therapy is not covered by insurance. Some medically indicated infusions (such as for diagnosed deficiencies) may have coverage — ask your provider. Most clinics offer transparent pricing, packages, and membership options to make regular sessions accessible.

What's the difference between IV therapy and an IV drip bar? A medically supervised IV clinic has licensed nurses and a supervising physician, performs health screenings, uses pharmacy-compounded formulas, and can manage any adverse reactions. An unsupervised drip bar carries more risk. Always choose medical oversight.

  • Myers' Cocktail — The gold standard. Magnesium, B vitamins, vitamin C, and calcium. Used for energy, immunity, migraines, fibromyalgia, and general wellness. Great starting point for first-timers.

  • Glutathione — The master antioxidant. Used for skin brightening, detoxification, liver support, and anti-aging.
  • Hydration Drip — Saline or lactated Ringer's with optional electrolytes. Fast relief for dehydration, illness, heat exhaustion, and hangover.
  • Athletic Recovery — Amino acids, B vitamins, magnesium, and anti-inflammatories to support muscle repair and reduce post-workout soreness.
  • Immune Boost — High-dose vitamin C, zinc, B12, and glutathione to fight off illness or support immune function proactively.
